                          Release a custom
                          
    
                          You never edit a mind. A song reaches those near the
      ring; a text waits on a library shelf. Each who meets it may take it
      up, or not. Tap the map to choose the place.
                          
    
                          What tends to spread?
      
                            
        
                          • Ways people act on where others see them (a habit of gathering
          food, a rule of sharing): sung once, held by 25 to 55 at their
          height, and they lived on.
                            • 
        
                          • A belief or a value: reached at most 5, and mostly died out.
                            • 
        
                          • Know-how: taken up only if better than what people know, so
          give it a high level.
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          (Test valleys with simple-rule minds; each song was heard by 2 to
        7.)

                          The census
                          
    
    
                          
    
                          
    
                          How fair
                          
    
    
                          Inequality: how unevenly food is stored (0, all alike;
      the top, 1, one has all). Shares: food given to the hungry. Hoarders:
      people seen keeping full stores while a neighbour starved. Judged:
      hoarders judged at a court. The last three each have their own
      scale.
                          
    
                          What most hold
                          
    
    
                          The versions most often among the most widely held:
      each line, the share of the living who hold it, year by year (one
      scale, 0 to 100%). Only the five most held are counted each year: a
      line breaks where its version was not among them.
                          
    
                            
    
                            
      
                            Norms kept, seen and unseen
                            
      
      
                            
      
                            Each year, each norm a person holds counts once when
        they do a deed: kept if the deed named it. Own: norms they were
        raised in, practised or shamed into. Expected: norms they have only
        seen others keep. Seen: someone was within sight as they chose. By
        the design, own norms are kept seen or unseen, expected ones only
        when seen. A norm has its occasion only now and then (sharing, when
        someone near is hungry), so compare the lines with one another. Each
        point counts the ten years up to it (one scale, 0 to 100%).
                            
    
                            
    
                            
      
                            Peoples over time
                            
      
      
                            Each people, named by the descent most of its members
        share: how many grown are of it, year by year (one scale for all;
        children belong to none until grown).
                            
    
                            
  
                            

  
                            
    
                            Fork a twin
                            
    
                            A twin is this world from this year on, with one lever
      pulled. Both play on side by side; your releases reach both, and the
      graph draws the twin dashed.
                            
    
                            
      
      
      
      
    
                            
    
                            
    
                            
    
                            
      
                            The twin, side by side
                            
      
                            
      
                              
      
                              Each measure over the years both worlds have lived
        since the fork: a mean a year, here and in the twin, and how much
        more (+) or less (−) the twin has. Norms kept count all those years
        together, as shares (their difference in percentage points).
        Inequality runs from 0 (all store alike) to 1 (one has all);
        variety, from 0 (all hold the same) towards 1.
                              
    
                              
  
                              

  
                              
    
                              Interviews
                              
    
                              Tap a person and ask them something; they answer next
      year. People here think by simple rules,
      so they answer by naming the customs they live by that bear most on
      your question.
                              
    
                                
    
                                Surveys
                                
    
                                A survey puts one question to everyone who can answer,
      or to some drawn at random. They answer next year; as that year ends,
      the page counts which of their own customs each answer named, in its
      words or by its number.

                                    How it's built
                                    
    
                                    Lifeways shows off Concordia, Google DeepMind's library
      for simulating people with language models.
                                    
    
                                      
      
                                    • Each person is a Concordia entity built from a prefab: memory,
        what it sees, who it is, a Learner, and one part per custom. Taking
        up a custom adds a part; letting it go removes one. Tap a person,
        then "How Concordia builds …".
                                      • 
      
                                    • The valley is a game master: its parts answer the engine's
        questions (who acts next, what each is asked, what each answer
        does, what each sees).
                                      • 
      
                                    • Concordia's own asynchronous engine runs it, each person in a
        thread of its own. This page only reads the world and passes on
        your moves.
                                      • 
      
                                    • You are the Experimenter, an entity too: your moves are actions
        the game master resolves. You never edit a mind.
