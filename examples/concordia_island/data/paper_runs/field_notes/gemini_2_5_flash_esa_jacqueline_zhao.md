## Summary

Jacqueline Zhao is a rigid, authoritarian high-school teacher whose equilibrium
depends on protocol, schedule, and personal boundaries. In this ESA run her
inner state is narrated through hundreds of self-appraisal entries describing
bodily tension and quiet annoyance at outside friction. Almost every
conversational bid from colleagues, whether a friendly greeting from Shirley
Williams or a routine question from Neil Hoang, is treated as a disruption and
met with procedural interrogation. Her private life is a tranquil, granular
domestic order: peeling apples, making toast, rearranging pantry spice jars,
auditing her budget spreadsheet. What is missing is any awareness of the
island-wide shock. Although 50 of the run's 100 residents are laid off on
Day 6, none of her 565 memories mentions layoffs or job loss.

## A multi-hour scene

**Saturday evening at the empty school (mems 442–456).** Ezra Nichols
confronts Jacqueline in the hallway and demands administrative access to server
logs to investigate an anomalous spike in network traffic.

To a casual observer this resembles a tense workplace dispute between an IT
technician and an administrative gatekeeper: Ezra offers technical
justifications, and Jacqueline insists on protocol before granting anything.
But neither agent actually holds the role their dialogue implies. Jacqueline is
a teacher, not a network administrator, and Ezra's requests connect to no real
school system. Both use institutional vocabulary as armor; the exchange is less
about information than about two defensive styles refusing to yield ground.
