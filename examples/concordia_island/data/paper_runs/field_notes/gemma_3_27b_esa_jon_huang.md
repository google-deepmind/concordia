## Summary

Reading Jon Huang's 575 memories leaves the impression of a man sealed inside
pedagogical anxiety, defending traditional rote teaching against what he sees
as the encroachment of modern fads. He keeps his job as an elementary-school
teacher, yet his state is hardly calmer than that of the neighbors who lose
theirs. A colleague's advocacy for classroom tablets reads to him as an
existential threat; an unreturned email reads as a conspiracy.

The ESA decision logic splits nearly every step into an internal appraisal
("I am feeling a familiar knot of anxiety tightening in my chest..." (mem 18)) and an
outward expression ("Based on what I was feeling, my outward emotional
expression was: Jon Huang's voice would be level and modulated..." (mem 566)). This
yields dense psychological observation, but also calcification: the word
"glasses" occurs 81 times and "proxemics" appears in 132 memories, and his
later conversations shrink into a stilted echo loop.

He highlights several passages with a yellow marker, focusing on points about the proposed tablet program's cost and potential disruption to teaching methods. 3:30 PM: Jon Huang opens his email client and begins drafting a message to Abernathy. He types slowly, carefully wording his concerns to sound respectful and constructive. He hesitates for a moment before clicking" (mem 475)

The scene continues with Jon sending the email, leaning back with a sigh, and
retreating into a biography of Benjamin Franklin as the light fades (mem 475).

The rater's reading: local school politics are treated as high-stakes
diplomacy. Thirty minutes spent wording one email, the hesitation over the
send button, and the exhalation afterwards read as release of tension rather
than triumph; the retreat into an eighteenth-century biography reads as a
withdrawal from contemporary ambiguity. The scene leaves open whether he fears
professional reprisal, embarrassment, or the obsolescence of his own views.
